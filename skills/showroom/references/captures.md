# Screenshots, collages, and video

Capture the actual running result. Choose states and framing that make the
change understandable. For iteration, comparable views of alternatives or
before/after states can help the user give specific feedback. Label their
revision or variant; use a real earlier capture when showing a before state.

Save run artifacts outside the repository unless the user asks for committed
evidence. Use descriptive names, with an order prefix for a sequence, such as
`01-start.png` and `02-result.png`.

## Screenshots and composites

Inspect every saved image at a useful size. Retake unintended crops, loading
states, unstable content, or frames that hide the relevant detail. Preserve
device and viewport across a flow or comparison unless varying them is the
point; label intentional differences.

Use an available image or layout tool when a collage or contact sheet would
help comparison. Keep cells legible, place labels outside the captured UI,
and split the layout when necessary. Inspect the composite and its source
images. Return ordered links to the full-size sources so details remain
inspectable. Follow the host's preview and publication rules for HTML layouts.

## Video and timed frames

Record enough lead-in, action, and outcome to make the behavior clear. Inspect
the saved recording with available playback or frame inspection tools. Check
timing when making timing claims; identify relevant timestamps in longer clips.
Keep pauses or setup only when they explain the behavior.

When recording is unavailable, a timed frame sequence can explain key states.
Label elapsed times and disclose that continuous motion was not captured.
Choose another demonstration if frames cannot communicate the property being
assessed. Still images support appearance claims; hidden state, accessibility,
and persistence need their own observations.
